Trademark Registration Cost in 2026: The Total Budget, Not Just USPTO Fees

Get Started Schedule a Call

October 7, 2026

"How much does it cost to trademark a name?" is usually the first question new business owners ask, and the answer you find online is often just the USPTO filing fee. That's only one piece of the budget. A realistic trademark registration cost estimate includes government fees, possible surcharges, professional fees, costs if problems arise, and maintenance fees to keep the registration alive. Here's the full picture under the USPTO fee schedule currently in effect.

1. USPTO Application Fees

Since January 18, 2025, the USPTO has used a single base application for electronically filed applications, replacing the older TEAS Plus and TEAS Standard options. The core fees:

FeeAmount
Base application$350 per class
Insufficient information surcharge$100 per class
Free-form text instead of Trademark ID Manual$200 per class
Each additional 1,000 characters of free-form text$200 per affected class

The most important cost driver is the number of classes. Each class of goods or services carries its own base fee and its own possible surcharges. A clothing brand that also sells bags and runs a retail store might need three classes, or $1,050 in base fees alone. For the details on surcharges and how to avoid them, see USPTO Trademark Filing Fees After 2025.

Example budgets (USPTO fees only)

  • One class, ID Manual entries, complete information: $350
  • Two classes, ID Manual entries: $700
  • One class with a custom (free-form) description under 1,000 characters: $550

2. Intent-to-Use Fees

If you file before using the mark, expect additional USPTO fees later in the process. See Intent-to-Use Applications.

  • Statement of Use or Amendment to Allege Use: $150 per class
  • Each six-month extension to file the Statement of Use: $125 per class

3. Clearance Search Costs

A free search of the USPTO database is a starting point, but it misses state registrations, unregistered common-law uses, and similar-sounding variations. A professional trademark search with attorney analysis costs money up front. It's often the best investment in the process, because a refusal or a dispute after launch costs far more.

4. Attorney Fees

Attorney fees vary by firm and by how much work is included. Common structures:

  • Flat fee for the application, sometimes bundled with a search
  • Separate flat or hourly fees for office action responses, depending on complexity
  • Flat fees for Statements of Use, extensions, and maintenance filings

When comparing quotes, ask what's included. Does it cover the search and the attorney's opinion? Drafting the identification to avoid surcharges? Monitoring deadlines? Responding to simple office actions? A low headline price that excludes these services may cost more in the end. Contact us for our current flat-fee options.

5. Costs if Problems Arise

Not every application sails through. Budget for the possibility of:

  • Office actions. Procedural issues can be inexpensive to fix. Substantive refusals, like likelihood of confusion or descriptiveness, require legal argument and cost more. See office action response.
  • Response extension. $125 to extend the office action response deadline by three months.
  • Appeal. A TTAB ex parte appeal currently carries a $225 per class fee, plus a brief fee.
  • Opposition. If a third party opposes your application, defending it is a contested proceeding with significantly higher legal costs. See trademark opposition.
  • Petition to revive. If an application is abandoned for a missed deadline, a petition to revive currently costs $250, if available.

6. Maintenance Costs

A federal trademark registration can last indefinitely, but only if you keep filing. Under the current schedule:

FilingWhenUSPTO fee
Section 8 declaration of useBetween years 5 and 6 after registration$325 per class
Section 15 declaration of incontestability (optional)After 5 years of continuous use$250 per class
Combined Section 8 & 9 renewalEvery 10 years$650 per class

Grace period filings carry additional fees. Missing a maintenance deadline can result in cancellation of the registration.

7. Florida State Registration as an Alternative or Supplement

A Florida state trademark under Chapter 495 costs $87.50 per class to file and $87.50 per class to renew every five years (s. 495.191). It's less expensive but protects the mark only in Florida and requires current use in the state. Compare options in Florida State Trademark vs. Federal Registration.

8. Hidden Costs of Not Registering

The cost of registration should be weighed against the cost of not registering: rebranding after a conflict, losing marketplace listings, lacking access to Amazon Brand Registry, or being unable to stop copycats efficiently. Read Why Trademark Registration Matters.

How to Keep Costs Predictable

  • Choose a distinctive mark. Descriptive marks draw refusals. See Choosing a Strong Trademark.
  • Search before filing.
  • File only in the classes you need now or have a genuine plan to enter.
  • Use Trademark ID Manual entries where they accurately fit.
  • Provide complete applicant information to avoid the $100 surcharge.
  • Use real specimens. See Specimen of Use Pitfalls.
  • Calendar every deadline, or have your attorney do it.

Beware of Scam Solicitations

After you file, your contact information becomes public. Many trademark owners receive official-looking letters and emails from private companies demanding payment for "registration," "monitoring," or "publication" services. The USPTO warns about these solicitations. Official USPTO correspondence comes from the USPTO's own domain, and fees are paid through USPTO systems. When in doubt, ask your attorney before paying anything.

The Bottom Line

For a straightforward one-class application, USPTO fees start at $350. A realistic total budget also includes a search, professional help, a cushion for office actions, and future maintenance filings. Planning for the full lifecycle avoids surprises. Ready to start?
